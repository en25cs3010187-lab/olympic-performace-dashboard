Problem - Solution Fit Template:
------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Problem-Solution Fit simply means that you have found a problem with your
customer and that the solution you have realized for it actually solves the
customer's problem. It helps entrepreneurs, marketers and corporate innovators
identify behavioral patterns and recognize what would work and why.

Purpose:
- Solve complex problems in a way that fits the state of your customers.
- Succeed faster and increase your solution adoption by tapping into existing
  mediums and channels of behavior.
- Sharpen your communication and marketing strategy with the right triggers
  and messaging.
- Increase touch-points with your company by finding the right problem-behavior
  fit and building trust by solving frequent annoyances, or urgent or costly
  problems.
- Understand the existing situation in order to improve it for your target group.

Note on evidence: The canvas below is based on assumptions about users. No user
research, interviews, or surveys were provided or verified, so the "fit" is a
design hypothesis and has Not been tested with real users.

Template (Problem-Solution Fit Canvas - 10 sections):

1. CUSTOMER SEGMENT(S)  [CS]
   Who is your customer?
   - Students and sports enthusiasts who want to understand Olympic medal
     performance.
   - Casual viewers (classmates, reviewers) who want an easy overview.
   - Beginner data analysts who want an example of Tableau dashboards inside a
     Flask website.
   (Assumed segments, not confirmed by research.)

2. JOBS-TO-BE-DONE / PROBLEMS  [J&P]
   - Find out which countries win the most medals.
   - See how medal performance changed over time.
   - Compare Summer and Winter Olympics, and compare regions.
   - Understand athlete participation by sport and gender distribution.
   - Follow the results in a clear order instead of many unrelated charts.

3. TRIGGERS  [TR]
   - A coursework or curiosity question such as "Which countries are strongest
     at the Olympics?"
   - Olympic news or discussions that make people want quick comparisons.
   - Receiving a link to the website.

4. EMOTIONS: BEFORE / AFTER  [EM]
   Before: overwhelmed by raw tables, unsure which numbers to trust, lost.
   After (intended): informed, curious to explore more, confident about the
   main patterns. Actual user emotions were Not measured.

5. AVAILABLE SOLUTIONS  [AS]
   - Searching for medal tables on websites and reading tables.
   - Building own charts in a spreadsheet.
   - Existing public dashboards or articles (specific ones were not reviewed
     for this document, so none are named).
   Pros: free and quick. Cons: tables are hard to compare; spreadsheets need
   time and skill; separate sources may not follow a guided story.

6. CUSTOMER CONSTRAINTS  [CC]
   - Limited time and limited data skills.
   - Needs a free tool that works in a normal browser on a laptop or phone.
   - Cannot easily check data quality or origin.

7. BEHAVIOUR  [BE]
   - Searches online for medal statistics.
   - Compares a few countries manually.
   - Prefers charts they can hover over and click.
   (Assumed behaviour, not researched.)

8. CHANNELS of BEHAVIOUR  [CH]
   8.1 ONLINE: web search, sharing links by messages, opening a Flask website
       with embedded Tableau Public dashboards in a browser.
   8.2 OFFLINE: classroom discussion and project presentations/reports where
       the live site link or screenshots are shown.

9. PROBLEM ROOT CAUSE  [RC]
   Olympic data covers many countries, editions, sports, and medal types, and
   it is usually presented as tables. Without visual summaries and a guided
   order, it is hard to see patterns. Combining sources with different levels
   of detail can also make counts unreliable (a data-quality risk that still
   needs to be checked).

10. YOUR SOLUTION  [SL]
    Global Olympic Sports Performance Analysis: interactive Tableau dashboards
    embedded in a Flask website with a home page and a /story page presenting
    four narrative points (Top 10 Countries by Medal Achievements; Medal
    Performance Over Time; Summer vs. Winter Olympics; Regional Medal
    Performance).
    - Live site: https://olympic-performance-dashboard.onrender.com
    - Dashboard 1: https://public.tableau.com/views/Olympic_Sport_Performance_Extract2/Dashboard1
    - Dashboard 2: https://public.tableau.com/views/Olympic_Sport_Performance_Extract3/Dashboard2
    - Code: https://github.com/aeraa1725-bot/olympic-performance-dashboard
    How well it fits user behaviour and constraints: Not verified with users.
    It matches the constraints in section 6 in design only (free to open,
    browser-based, guided story page).
